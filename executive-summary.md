
# Executive Summary
## Healthcare Information Security Risk Assessment – Hopewell Medical Centre

**Prepared by:** Oluebube Charity Nwaejike  
**Professional Role:** Cybersecurity GRC Professional  
**Project Type:** Illustrative GRC Portfolio Assessment  
**Organisation:** Hopewell Medical Centre (Fictional)  
**Assessment Focus:** Healthcare Information Security

---

## 1. Overview

This document presents an executive summary of an illustrative Information Security Risk Assessment conducted for Hopewell Medical Centre, a fictional healthcare organisation.

The assessment demonstrates a structured approach to identifying information security risks, evaluating their potential impact on organisational operations and patient information, and recommending appropriate risk treatment measures.

Healthcare organisations handle sensitive patient information and depend on information systems to support medical records management, patient registration, laboratory operations, and administrative activities.

Protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of these information assets is therefore essential to maintaining patient trust, operational continuity, and organisational accountability.

## 2. Assessment Objectives

The objectives of this assessment were to:

- Identify critical information assets within the organisation.
- Identify potential information security threats and vulnerabilities.
- Evaluate risks based on their likelihood and potential impact.
- Establish risk priorities using a consistent assessment methodology.
- Recommend appropriate risk treatment measures.
- Support management decision-making and continuous improvement of information security.

## 3. Assessment Scope

The assessment considered 10 information assets across relevant organisational functions, including:

- Electronic Health Records.
- Patient Registration Database.
- Staff Login Credentials.
- Hospital Computer Systems.
- Hospital Network Infrastructure.
- Laboratory Information Records.
- Data Backup Storage.
- Staff Personnel Records.
- Security Policies and Procedures.
- Third-Party Service Provider Records.

The assessment focused on information security risks relating to unauthorised access, data disclosure, malware, network compromise, data unavailability, insider threats, phishing, policy governance, and third-party security.

## 4. Assessment Methodology

A qualitative risk assessment approach was adopted using a 5×5 Likelihood and Impact Matrix.

Each identified risk was evaluated using:

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

The resulting scores were classified as:

| Risk score | Risk rating |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low |
| 5–9 | Moderate |
| 10–16 | High |
| 17–25 | Critical |

The assessment methodology was informed by relevant principles from:

- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 – Information Security Management Systems.
- ISO/IEC 27005 – Information Security Risk Management.
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0.

The scoring thresholds represent illustrative project-specific criteria and are not universal requirements prescribed by these frameworks.

## 5. Key Risk Assessment Findings

A total of 10 information security risks were identified and evaluated.

The initial risk distribution was:

| Risk rating | Number of risks |
|---|---:|
| Critical | 2 |
| High | 7 |
| Moderate | 1 |
| Low | 0 |
| **Total** | **10** |

### Critical Risks

Two risks received Critical ratings:

1. Unauthorised disclosure of patient records due to potential weaknesses in access controls.
2. Compromise of staff login credentials due to weak authentication practices and inadequate account protection.

These risks require priority management attention because of their potential consequences for patient confidentiality, organisational operations, and trust.

### High Risks

Seven risks received High ratings, covering:

- Malware infection resulting from unpatched systems.
- Network compromise and lateral movement.
- Ransomware and data unavailability.
- Excessive staff access privileges.
- Unauthorised access to laboratory information.
- Phishing-related compromise of employee information or accounts.
- Third-party security weaknesses and potential data exposure.

### Moderate Risk

One risk received a Moderate rating:

- Unauthorised modification of security policies due to inadequate document access and version control.

Although rated Moderate, this risk should still be addressed through appropriate governance measures.

## 6. Recommended Risk Treatment Priorities

The assessment recommends prioritising the following security improvements:

**1. Strengthen identity and access management**
- Implement Multi-Factor Authentication.
- Introduce role-based access control.
- Apply least-privilege principles.
- Conduct periodic access reviews.

**2. Improve system and network security**
- Establish a structured patch management programme.
- Deploy appropriate endpoint protection.
- Conduct vulnerability assessments.
- Implement network segmentation and firewall reviews.

**3. Strengthen backup and recovery arrangements**
- Maintain encrypted offline or immutable backups.
- Restrict backup access.
- Conduct periodic backup restoration tests.

**4. Improve staff security awareness**
- Conduct regular cybersecurity awareness training.
- Introduce phishing simulation exercises.
- Establish clear incident reporting procedures.

**5. Strengthen third-party risk management**
- Conduct security assessments of service providers.
- Include appropriate security and data protection requirements in contracts.
- Review third-party access privileges.

**6. Improve security governance**
- Establish document access restrictions.
- Introduce policy approval and version-control procedures.
- Monitor treatment activities and report outstanding risks to management.

## 7. Management Recommendations

Management is encouraged to:

1. Review and approve the proposed Risk Treatment Plan.
2. Assign accountable risk owners and treatment action owners.
3. Allocate appropriate financial, technical, and human resources.
4. Prioritise remediation of Critical and High risks.
5. Establish a formal risk acceptance and escalation process.
6. Require periodic reporting on risk treatment progress.
7. Support continuous monitoring and reassessment of information security risks.

Risk acceptance decisions should be documented and authorised in accordance with the organisation's approved risk appetite and governance arrangements.

## 8. Conclusion

The assessment demonstrates that healthcare information security requires a coordinated approach involving technology, people, policies, and management oversight.

The identification of two Critical risks and seven High risks highlights the importance of strengthening access controls, protecting patient information, improving system resilience, and maintaining effective security governance.

Implementing the proposed risk treatment measures would provide a structured basis for reducing information security exposure and improving organisational security maturity.

Management commitment, clear accountability, adequate resources, and continuous monitoring will be essential to achieving these objectives.

This is an illustrative cybersecurity GRC portfolio project developed for demonstration and professional development purposes.

Hopewell Medical Centre is fictional. No actual organisational audit, technical testing, stakeholder interviews, control validation, or management approval was performed.

The assets, risks, scores, recommendations, and treatment timelines are hypothetical assumptions.

The risk ratings represent illustrative initial risk assessments. Actual risk levels and treatment effectiveness would require validation through evidence collection, stakeholder engagement, and ongoing monitoring.

This document should not be interpreted as evidence of ISO/IEC 27001 certification or compliance by any real healthcare organisation.
